# Strategy: mathematics, approximations and sources

Every decision the agent returns carries its own `basis` lines tagged with one of four labels. This document says what each means and where the numbers come from.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `precomputed-chart` | read from a chart shipped in the repository |
| `computed` | calculated at decision time (Monte Carlo, arithmetic) |
| `learned` | derived from this opponent's observed statistics |
| `heuristic` | a hand-set approximation; not solver output |

Nothing in the bot is a game-theory-optimal solution, and nothing is described as one. The preflop charts are third-party or hand-encoded approximations; the postflop engine is a two-round look-ahead under a heuristic opponent model.

**Version note (0.6.0).** The postflop engine and the opponent model were replaced after an audit against recorded hands. Sections 3 (postflop part) and 6 describe the old engine, kept as the benchmark baseline; **section 8 describes what runs now**. Audit: [STRATEGY_AUDIT.md](STRATEGY_AUDIT.md). Strategy data and import formats: [STRATEGY_LIBRARY.md](STRATEGY_LIBRARY.md).

## 1. How heads-up versus multiway is chosen

Three independent facts are read from the reconstructed hand (`tableCounts` in `src/bot/table/engine.ts`):

- `dealtIn`: players dealt into this hand.
- `opponentsContesting`: opponents who have not folded, all-in players included.
- `canAct`: players who can still make betting decisions.

The agent then picks one of three branches (`mode` in every decision):

| Branch | Condition | Consequences |
|---|---|---|
| `heads-up-table` | `dealtIn == 2` | Button posts the small blind, acts first preflop and last postflop. Preflop uses the heads-up charts (button opens about 80%). |
| `heads-up-pot` | `dealtIn >= 3` and one opponent left | Ring-game positions and ring preflop ranges (a big blind facing an under-the-gun open is not a heads-up-table spot). Equity and EV simulate one opponent with the range implied by that player's actual line. |
| `multiway` | two or more opponents left | Every opponent is sampled jointly in each Monte Carlo deal; bluff rates, continue thresholds and the realization factor all tighten with the number of opponents. |

The specification's scenarios map as follows: A (ten seated, eight fold) is `heads-up-pot`; B is `multiway` with three opponents; C (three seated, one folds) is `heads-up-pot`; D is `heads-up-table`; E (one all-in, two with chips) is `multiway` with `contesting = 3`, `canAct = 2`, and the all-in player stays in the equity calculation with a fixed investment while only the player with chips is modelled as responding to bets.

Positions come from the button and the seats dealt in (`table/positions.ts`), never from seat numbers. Each seat also gets `behindPreflop`, the number of players left to act in an unopened pot, which is what the preflop ranges key on.

## 2. Hand evaluation and equity

**Evaluator** (`src/core/equity/hand-eval.ts`, reused): perfect-hash seven-card evaluator ported from phevaluator (Apache-2.0). The original repository verifies it by enumerating all 2,598,960 five-card hands.

**Multiway equity** (`src/bot/equity/equity.ts`): Hero's equity is the expected share of the pot at showdown,

```
equity = E[ 1/k if Hero is among the k best hands, else 0 ]
```

over the joint distribution of all opponents' holdings and the runout. Each opponent `i` has a weighted range `w_i(combo)`; the joint distribution is `P(c_1..c_N) ∝ Π w_i(c_i) · [all cards distinct]`, sampled by drawing every opponent independently and rejecting the whole tuple on any shared card (`MultiwayDealSampler`). Multiway equity is never built from heads-up equities.

Reported with it: win probability, tie probability, sample count and the standard error `sqrt(var/n)`. `ProgressiveEquity` can be refined in slices.

**Side pots** (`showdownPayout`): given what every player has invested and each showdown rank, the pot is cut into levels at each distinct investment; each level is paid to the best hand among players who reached it; folded players' chips are dead money in the levels they reached; an uncalled excess returns to its owner. This is used inside the EV simulation, so Hero is only credited with pots it is eligible for.

## 3. Opponent ranges

### Preflop (`ranges/preflop-model.ts`), heuristic

The 169 starting hands are ordered by `PREFLOP_STRENGTH` (`src/core/ranges/preflop-strength.ts`, generated by the repository: half all-in equity versus a random hand, half versus the top 20%). It is an equity ordering, so it undervalues suited connectors and overvalues weak offsuit aces relative to solver ranges.

An opponent's line is classified (open, isolation raise, limp, cold call, big-blind defend, 3-bet, call 3-bet, 4-bet, jam, and so on) and mapped to percentile bands. Opening width by players left to act:

| Behind | 1 (SB) | 2 (BTN) | 3 (CO) | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opens | 42% | 44% | 28% | 21% | 17% | 14.5% | 12.5% | 11% | 10% |

Heads-up table button: 80%. Other lines are fractions of the relevant opener's width (for example a 3-bet is the top `0.3 × opener width`, clamped to 2–14%, plus a thin bluff band). Jam ranges come from a stack-depth curve (75% at 2bb down to 2.5% at 100bb), tightened for extra players behind. These widths are round approximations of commonly published frequencies, not solver output.

### Postflop (`ranges/postflop-model.ts`), heuristic

For a board, every holding gets:

- `hs`: percentile of its made hand among all possible holdings (1 = nuts),
- `outs`: 9 for a flush draw, 4 per straight-completing rank up to 8, capped at 15,
- `es = hs + (1 − hs) · P(hit) · 0.85`, the effective strength.

Behaviour is logistic in `es`:

- **Continue threshold** versus a bet of `x` pots: `0.22 + 0.32·√x`, plus 0.06 on the river, 0.10 facing a raise, 0.03 per extra opponent, minus `0.12 × stickiness`.
- **Bet probability**: a value part `σ((es − t_v)/0.06)` with `t_v` rising with bet size, opponents and raises, plus a bluff part (30% flop, 20% turn, 12% river of non-value holdings; divided for multiway pots; scaled by `aggression`).
- **Raise probability**: mostly holdings above 0.90 (0.95 facing a raise) plus a small bluff rate.

After each observed action the range is updated by Bayes' rule: `weight ← weight × P(action | holding)`. Hero's cards and the board are removed. The same functions predict how opponents respond to Hero's candidate bets, so narrowing and prediction cannot contradict each other.

## 4. Opponent statistics (`opponents/profile.ts`)

Every statistic is a pair `{n, d}`: times done over times there was a real opportunity. Examples of the opportunity rules: the opener cannot 3-bet its own open; a big blind that wins a walk has no VPIP opportunity; fold-to-c-bet counts only the first response to the preflop aggressor's first bet, not a spot where a raise intervened.

Tracked: VPIP, PFR, first-in raise, limp, 3-bet, cold call, fold to 3-bet, 4-bet, c-bet flop and turn, fold to c-bet flop and turn, check-raise, aggression frequency, bets-and-raises versus calls, went to showdown, won at showdown; fold / call / raise versus a bet on each street; bet when checked to on each street; fold versus bet in heads-up and multiway pots; VPIP and first-in raise by early / late / blind position; bet-size histogram; strength of revealed hands by bet size; recent showdowns.

**Estimate.** Each statistic has a Beta prior with mean `m` and strength `k` pseudo-observations (population priors, heuristic; listed in `PRIORS`). The estimate is `(m·k + n) / (k + d)` with an approximate 90% interval from the Beta variance. Three hands of 100% VPIP therefore estimate about 41%, not 100%.

**Use.** Four knobs feed the range and response model: looseness and preflop raising (multipliers on preflop widths), aggression (bluff and bet rates) and stickiness (continue thresholds). Each deviation from neutral is additionally capped by `d/(d + 20)` and by the `exploitWeight` setting.

**Explicit exploits** need evidence: the 90% interval must clear the threshold with a minimum number of opportunities (for example folds to 3-bets: at least 10 opportunities and a lower bound above 62%). Only one such exploit changes hero's chart play today (light 3-bets against a proven over-folder); the others inform the response model through the knobs.

**Classification.** Probabilities of tight/loose × passive/aggressive from the normal approximation to the posteriors, with an "unclassified" mass `exp(−hands/20)`. They are for display; decisions use the statistics.

A hand is applied at most once per profile (hand-id list in the profile, plus a single IndexedDB transaction keyed by hand id).

## 5. Preflop decisions (`strategy/preflop.ts`)

Checked in this order:

1. **Facing an all-in, or a bet that commits 40% of the stack** (`computed`): equity against the estimated ranges of every committed opponent by Monte Carlo, then `EV(call) = equity × winnable − toCall`, where `winnable` caps every contribution at Hero's own stack. Call when EV exceeds a margin of 1.5% of the pot plus 2% per player still to act. Chip EV only: no ICM.
2. **12bb or less, unopened**: jam or fold. With exactly one player behind, the heads-up push/fold Nash chart (`src/core/ranges/pushfold-nash.ts`, `precomputed-chart`). Otherwise the stack-depth jam curve tightened for players behind (`heuristic`).
3. **16bb or less facing a raise**: re-jam the top `0.5 × opener width` or fold; the big blind may call a cheap raise.
4. **Heads-up table**: `src/core/ranges/preflop-charts.ts` (`precomputed-chart`). These are hand-encoded heads-up ranges for about 100bb, an approximation of equilibrium play, pure strategies.
5. **Ring game**: the Greenline and Pekarstas 6-max chart packs (`src/core/ranges/greenline-gto.ts`, `pekarstas-gto.ts`, `precomputed-chart`; third-party charts for about 100bb whose solver provenance this repository cannot verify). A seat uses the 6-max position with the same number of players behind: the last six seats of any table map to UTG, MP, CO, BTN, SB, BB. Covered: first-in raise, isolation, facing an open, facing a 3-bet, facing a 4-bet, with mixed frequencies sampled by a seeded generator. Chart cells that are near-uniform three-way mixes are treated as folds (they are unconverged nodes in the source).
6. **Anything the charts do not cover** (`heuristic`): seats earlier than 6-max UTG at 7 to 10 handed tables, squeezes with callers, cold 4-bets, limped pots. Percentile rules from the table in section 3.

Sizing: open 2.5bb (3bb from the small blind) plus 1bb per limper; 3-bet 3× the open in position, 4× from the blinds, plus one open per caller; 4-bet 2.3× or 2.7×. A raise that would put in 45% of the stack becomes all-in.

Charts are for roughly 100bb. They are still used at 16 to 60bb, which is an approximation the benchmark does not isolate.

## 6. Postflop decisions (`strategy/postflop.ts`), computed under the heuristic model

**Candidates**: fold, check or call, and bets of 33%, 66%, 100% and 150% of the pot (or raises of 55% and 100% of the pot after calling), plus all-in when it is at most 2.5 pots. Sizes are clamped to the legal window and to what the deepest opponent can call.

**Per sampled deal** (one holding per opponent drawn jointly, plus a runout), for each candidate:

- **Fold**: 0.
- **Call**: opponents still to act behind fold or continue by their response probabilities; survivors go to showdown through the side-pot payout. `value = R × payout − toCall`.
- **Check**: an opponent behind may bet 60% of the pot with its bet probability; Hero then continues only if its own strength clears the continue threshold; otherwise the hand goes to showdown.
- **Bet or raise to T**: each opponent folds, calls or raises by its response probabilities given its sampled holding. All fold: Hero wins the pot. A raise: Hero continues only if `clamp((es_hero − 0.78)/0.2, 0, 1)` covers the pot odds, otherwise loses the bet. Otherwise showdown among Hero and the callers.

`R` is the **realization factor**, a heuristic that stands in for the betting that is not simulated: 1 on the river and whenever no further betting is possible; otherwise 0.92 on the flop and 0.96 on the turn, minus 0.07 / 0.04 out of position, minus 0.02 per extra opponent, plus 0.06 for very strong hands, plus 0.03 when Hero bet and was called.

**EV** is the sample mean, in chips, relative to folding now. The same random numbers are used for all candidates, so differences between candidates are far less noisy than the EVs themselves; the covariance matrix gives the standard error of each difference.

**Pessimistic second scenario.** Every bet or raise is also evaluated against opponents with stickiness raised by 0.4, and the candidate is scored by the lower of the two EVs. A bluff must therefore still be profitable against an opponent who folds less than the model says. Without this the engine bluff-raised too readily on thin model evidence.

**Mixing.** The highest EV is chosen. Other candidates are mixed in only when they are within `max(1.2% of the pot, the standard error of the difference)`, with weight `exp(−gap / (0.5 × tolerance))`. The draw is seeded per decision point so refinement cannot flip the choice by re-rolling.

**What the standard errors do and do not mean.** They measure Monte Carlo noise. They do not measure the error of the opponent model, the realization factor or the one-round horizon, which is larger and unquantified. The uncertainty line in each decision says so.

Known biases of this method: it does not plan multi-street lines (no slow-play credit, no implied odds beyond `R`); when raised it assumes other callers give up; a raise behind a call is treated as a call; and an opponent who has "closed" the action is assumed not to bet again this street.

## 7. Decision time (`time/budget.ts`)

Importance is `streetWeight × (0.45·stakes + 0.3·branching + 0.25·multiway) × (0.5 + 0.5·confidence)`, where stakes is `log2(1 + min(effective stack, 2·pot + 2 bb)) / log2(101)`. The chips at risk are capped by the effective stack, so a huge pot with little behind does not buy extra time.

- soft limit `60 + 900 × importance` ms, hard limit `250 + 3200 × importance` ms, both capped by the action clock minus a 2.5 s safety margin;
- at least 300 samples, at most `4000 + 36000 × importance`.

The loop returns a provisional decision after the first batch and stops when: the sample ceiling or hard limit is reached; the best action leads by more than 2.5 standard errors and 0.5% of the pot; the candidates are tied within 0.4% of the pot (more samples cannot matter); or, past the soft limit, the difference is known to within 0.3% of the pot. It is cancelled the moment the hand or the turn changes.

In autonomous mode a bounded random think time (settings, default 0.6 to 2.2 s) is added, never past the deadline. When the page shows no clock, the configured assumed clock (default 20 s) is used.

Measured: a decision takes a few milliseconds even at 8,000 samples (see `reports/`), so in practice the stopping rule, not the clock, ends the computation.

## 8. Version 2 (0.6.0): what replaced sections 3 and 6

Sections 3 (postflop model) and 6 above describe **version 1**, the engine up to 0.5.1. It is still in the repository (`strategy/postflop-v1.ts`, `ranges/postflop-model.ts`, `engine: 'v1'`) as the benchmark baseline and is not used for live recommendations. The reasons it was replaced, with the recorded hands that show them, are in [STRATEGY_AUDIT.md](STRATEGY_AUDIT.md). This section describes what runs now.

### 8.1 Spot analysis (`strategy/spot.ts`)

Read from the reconstructed action sequence, so it reflects what Hero actually did, including actions taken against advice:

- last preflop raiser, pot type (limped, single-raised, 3-bet, 4-bet or more);
- **betting lead**: the last bettor or raiser of the street immediately before this one, if still in the hand. A street checked through passes no lead on;
- Hero's position against the players left: in position, out of position, in between;
- stack-to-pot ratio;
- the name of a bet made now: continuation bet (Hero holds the lead), lead or donk bet (the player with the lead is still to act behind), probe bet (the lead was given up), delayed continuation bet, raise;
- whether Hero's two cards are in the range the model gives Hero's own preflop line. If not, the decision is marked **out-of-distribution**.

### 8.2 Ranges

Preflop ranges for opponents come from the strategy library where a pack covers the line (open, isolation raise, call or 3-bet of an open, call or 4-bet of a 3-bet), otherwise from the percentile bands of section 3. See [STRATEGY_LIBRARY.md](STRATEGY_LIBRARY.md).

The same is done for Hero's seat. The result is Hero's **perceived range**: what a player who took Hero's line holds. It is used only to model how opponents see Hero. Hero's own decision is always computed for the two cards actually held.

### 8.3 Strength inside ranges (`ranges/range-model.ts`), heuristic weights on computed quantities

For a player with range `R` facing opponents whose ranges mix to `M`, on a given board:

```
qOwn(hand) = share of R (by weight) with lower effective strength
qOpp(hand) = share of M with lower effective strength
s(hand)    = 0.35 · qOwn + 0.35 · qOpp + 0.30 · es        (es as in section 3)
```

For Hero's actual hand `s = 0.70 · qOpp + 0.30 · es`, because the hand need not be in Hero's perceived range.

All thresholds apply to `s`. Between equal ranges they read as fractions of a range. When one range is stronger on a board, `qOpp` lifts all of its hands and lowers all of the other's: the stronger range bets more and folds less. That is how range advantage enters; no board type is hard-coded.

Behaviour, per holding:

- **Bet when able to bet first**: value part `σ((s − t_v)/0.05)` with `t_v = 1 − valueTop + 0.1·(x − 0.6) + 0.04·(opponents − 1)`, plus a bluff share of the rest, divided for extra opponents and scaled by the opponent's measured aggression.

  | | flop | turn | river |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | value share, no lead | 28% | 27% | 27% |
  | bluff share, no lead | 17% | 15% | 11% |
  | value share, with the lead | 34% | 30% | 30% |
  | bluff share, with the lead | 32% | 21% | 14% |

  Into the lead (the previous aggressor still to act behind) the whole probability is multiplied by 0.3.
- **Facing a bet of x pots**: continues above `0.22 + 0.32·√x` (+0.06 river, +0.10 facing a raise, +0.03 per extra opponent, −0.25 × stickiness); raises mostly above 0.90.
- **Narrowing**: Bayes' rule with these probabilities, using the range as it stands before each action. A check never multiplies a holding by less than 0.2 (slow-plays). If less than 3% of a range survives a line, part of the starting range is mixed back and the decision is marked "poorly explained by the behaviour model".

The frequencies were chosen so that two equal ranges reproduce the population priors of section 4 (continuation bet 55% flop and 45% turn, bet when checked to 40 / 38 / 35%, fold to a bet about 45%). `tests/bot/strategy-v2.test.ts` checks the flop figures on eight boards. They are round figures, not measurements and not solver output. Stickiness now applies a player's measured fold rate at face value (shrunk for small samples).

### 8.4 Decision (`strategy/postflop.ts`), a calculated approximation

Candidates are as in section 6. For each sampled deal and each candidate:

1. **This street.** Opponents fold, call or raise by 8.3.
2. **Hero's follow-up is a plan.** "Check then fold to a bet" and "check then call a bet" are valued separately over all deals; the better is the value of checking. Likewise "bet then fold to a raise" and "bet then call a raise". Consequence: `EV(check) ≥ 0` always.
3. **Next street** (flop to turn, turn to river). The sampled card is dealt and one betting round is played by everyone left, Hero included: in action order the first player willing to bet (8.3, with the lead belonging to whoever bet last on this street) bets half the pot, the others fold or call. A player who continued against a bet on this street is re-read inside the top `1 − t` of its range. Hero's simulated play bets for value but almost never bluffs. Every line is valued twice, with Hero following the model against a bet on that street and with Hero giving up, and the better counts.
4. **Showdown** with exact side pots. After a flop decision the river betting is still not simulated: the payout is multiplied by the turn realization factor of section 6. When no betting is possible (someone is all-in), no factor is applied, so an all-in call is exactly `equity × pot − price`.

Every bet or raise is also valued against opponents who fold about ten points less (stickiness +0.4) and scored by the lower of the two values. Mixing and standard errors are as in section 6.

The explanation reports, from the same simulation: bet type and purpose (value bet if Hero wins at least half the time against the hands that call; protection or thin value; thin bet; bluff or semi-bluff below one third), the share of deals in which everyone folds, someone calls, someone raises, Hero's showdown share against the callers, the plan, and how often an opponent bets after a check.

**Lower confidence** is stated when: Hero's hand is out-of-distribution; the chosen bet wins under a third of the time when called (it rests on the modelled fold rate); or an opponent's line fits the model poorly.

Known limits of this method: one more street, not the whole tree; one bet size for opponents and for every simulated later bet; no check-raise by Hero and no raise behind a call; hand-set frequencies; no solver reference. In pilot simulations it cost about four to six times the computation of version 1.

### 8.5 Preflop

Section 5 still describes the order of checks. Changes: chart lookups go through the library and cite the pack, its verification status and every departure from its assumptions; the big blind over limpers uses the chart's raising hands; the width of a raise Hero faces is the width of the range modelled for the raiser's actual line, not the first-in width of the raiser's seat.

## 9. Version 3 (0.8.0, experimental, off by default): search over betting sequences

Selected with Settings → Strategy experiments → Version 3, or `engine: 'v3'`. It replaces version 2 only in heads-up pots after the flop; preflop decisions and pots with two or more opponents are version 2, and the explanation says so. Full account, audit and measurements: [PHASE_2_3_ADVANCED_STRATEGY.md](PHASE_2_3_ADVANCED_STRATEGY.md).

### 9.1 What it computes (`strategy/v3/search.ts`), a calculated approximation

1. **Public tree.** The current street and the next street are expanded with the rules engine itself, so minimum raises, the re-open rule, all-ins and uncalled bets are the game's own rules. Discrete sizes: Hero's first action uses version 2's sizes (bets of 33/66/100/150% of the pot and all-in when the stack is short; raises of 55 and 100% of the pot after calling); later Hero raises are about 45% and 90% of the pot after calling, plus all-in; the opponent bets 33% or 75% of the pot and raises about pot. At most four bets and raises on the current street and three on the next. Sizes are whole chips at stakes of two or more chips per big blind and cents below.
2. **Deals.** The opponent's holding is sampled from version 2's range estimate (section 8.2) with the rest of the board. Its strength on this street and on the next board, and the showdown result, are computed once per deal.
3. **Opponent nodes** follow the history-conditional model of 9.2 with the strength of the holding dealt. Each deal carries the probability that the opponent took every action on the path (its reach), so the opponent's range after any line is exactly the holdings that the model plays that way.
4. **Hero nodes.** Hero takes the action with the highest reach-weighted value over the deals that reach the node: on the current street one choice per node, on the next street one per node and per strength bucket of Hero's hand on the new card (four buckets). Hero never conditions on the opponent's cards. This is a best response to the opponent model on this tree.
5. **Leaves.** Fold: exact. River or all-in showdown: exact, with side pots. End of the next street with a card still to come: the showdown share times the realization factor of section 6.
6. **Model uncertainty.** Each deal also draws which opponent Hero faces: the modelled one, a stickier one (stickiness +0.4) or a more aggressive one (aggression +0.6, raises doubled). Before any evidence the two deviations have 20% each; their share falls as the opponent's own responses to bets are observed (×20 / (20 + opportunities)). Hero cannot tell them apart, so its choices are a best response to the mixture. This replaces version 2's "score every bet by the lower of two values".

Reported for the chosen action: the plan (what Hero does if raised; after a check, what Hero does against each bet size, called a check-raise when it is a raise); for bets, the value contributed by folds, calls and raises; for checks, the value of every answer to each bet size and the purpose of a check-raise computed from the equity against the hands that continue and the share that folds (value, protection or thin value, semi-bluff when the hand's equity comes from a draw, bluff); the value of the chosen action against each opponent of the mixture on its own; and every opponent knob that moved, with its evidence.

### 9.2 Opponent model (`strategy/v3/model.ts`), heuristic

Version 2's functions (section 8.3) with thresholds that depend on the betting so far:

| The opponent faces | Continue threshold | Raise probability |
|---|---|---|
| a bet | version 2 | version 2 × the player's raise tendency |
| a raise of its own bet | + 0.04 (`foldVsRaise` evidence moves it) | × 0.6 |
| a check-raise | + 0.06 (`foldToCheckRaise` evidence moves it) | × 0.6 |
| a third raise or more | + 0.08 more | × 0.3 |
| may bet first | version 2 bet probability, × turn-barrel tendency when it holds the lead, × river-bet tendency on the river | bet size: the larger size with the player's own large-bet share (prior 45%), a little more often with the strongest and weakest holdings |

Player knobs come from `opponents/profile.ts` with shrinkage: each moves by (posterior − prior) × d / (d + 20), so 20 opportunities count half. New statistics in 0.8.0: `foldToCheckRaise`, `foldVsRaise`, `reraiseVsRaise`.

### 9.3 Limits

Two streets, not the whole hand; discrete sizes; hand-set thresholds; the opponent's strength on the next board is measured inside its starting range (the reach weights carry the narrowing); next-street choices are made on the same deals they are valued on, which slightly favours lines that reach the next street; heads-up only. About 60 ms per decision at 400 deals (median), 100 ms at the 95th percentile, in the simulator.
